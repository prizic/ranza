Ranza · yurt-first audit · 31 Oct 2026 · hosted staging a78da726, code re-checked at origin/main 897d7fd0

Can a Turkish yurt run on Ranza today? Not for one month.

Verdict. The interface is a well-built hotel front desk, and a dormitory operator would feel that on every screen. Three things stop a private öğrenci yurdu from running a month on it, and none of them is visual. First, nothing bills a Resident, so a yurt on Ranza has no income line. Second, the student record is a name, an email and a phone: no kimlik or passport, no gender, no guardian (veli), no university. Third, no screen thinks in terms of beds over a term: the calendar stops at 30 days, a room card shows one person, and a bed is priced the same in a single room as in a six-bed room. Going yurt-first is the right call if there is a named yurt operator to design with. Without one, the billing design (monthly rent or an annual fee in taksit/senet instalments) is a guess, and it is the most expensive thing to get wrong.

What "yurt" means here. I read "yurt systems" as Turkish student dormitories (özel öğrenci yurtları), not glamping tents. That is what the word means in Turkish, this repository began as a student-dormitory pilot (ADR 0004: "a dormitory operator remains a live prospect"), and the design references use "Turkish student-housing data" (docs/design/visual-reference.md). If glamping yurts were meant, sections 4 and 7 change completely; the billing, Guest-record and calendar findings in section 3 would still apply.
This is a priority change, not a scope change. Blueprint section 1 already lists student residences, and ADR 0004 already says a dormitory is a Property configuration, not a fork. So "yurt first" needs no blueprint amendment. It needs a roadmap reorder and, for the configuration switch in section 6, a new ADR written as Status: Proposed. Do not answer it with a "yurt edition": ADR 0004 forbids that, and it is right to.

4. Vocabulary: the Turkish copy speaks hotel

A yurt manager reads the product in Turkish, and the Turkish is a hotel's. The canonical terms stay as they are in code (blueprint section 2: Resident, Guest, Property). What changes is the Turkish label a dormitory Property shows. That is the "approved terminology mappings" white-label setting in blueprint section 8, which is not built.

5. The Turkish dormitory context

Fact What it means for Ranza
Private higher-education dormitories are governed by the Yükseköğrenim Özel Barınma Hizmetleri Yönetmeliği, Gençlik ve Spor Bakanlığı, Resmi Gazete 31948, 9 Sept 2022 FROM SEARCH, TEXT NOT READ This is the document to read before designing registration, attendance and parent notification. Its clauses on those topics were not found in the search; counsel or the customer should point at them.
Ministry dormitories notify a student's leave to the guardian by SMS or email when the student is under 18 FROM SEARCH, STATE YURTS ONLY Private yurts commonly copy this practice. Veli notification is a likely day-one ask, and it needs the messaging foundation that does not exist.
Whether private yurts must report residents through KBS UNKNOWN The search found no clear statement. A 2010 rule had dormitories notify student identities to the education directorate within five days; the authority has since moved to GSB/KYK. Ask counsel before building either a KBS or a ministry export.
The government has capped private-dormitory fee increases HEADLINE ONLY A price list with history and an effective date matters more for a yurt than for a hotel. Ranza's rate change already records who changed it and when.
Turkish yurt programs (Hursoft Yurt Programı, Microm Yurt Otomasyonu) centre on student, veli and kefil (guarantor) records, leave (izin) and senet (promissory-note) instalment tracking FROM LISTINGS This is the local bar, and it is low: desktop programs from download sites. Ranza wins on the web, the portal, roles and audit, but only once it has the student record and instalments those programs treat as basics.

6. Architecture stance

A "dormitory" operating model on the Property, as configuration. One setting chosen when the Property is configured. It sets defaults and never forks behaviour:

  • the default stay type in the booking form becomes Resident, and term presets replace free dates;
  • the Turkish labels in section 4 (and the Arabic and English equivalents) apply;
  • Today shows the dormitory tiles and the calendar opens on the term;
  • Close the day stays available for summer Guests but stops being the daily ritual.

The rules underneath stay the same, which is ADR 0004's whole point. The alternative, scattering if (stayType === "resident") through each screen, is what the screens already do. It is why the product looks hotel-shaped while technically supporting Residents. This wants a Proposed ADR before any code.

Billing as an instalment schedule, not "monthly rent". The general case is a contract with N instalments, each with an amount and a due date, posted to the Folio by the worker on its due date. Monthly is N = 10 or 12; an annual fee paid in four senets is N = 4; pay-in-full is N = 1. Building "monthly" alone would be the specialisation the project has decided to defer (ADR 0004 and the general-case-first rule). It reuses money as integer minor units, corrections as reversal lines (ADR 0015) and the outbox (ADR 0017).

A bed's price follows its room. Today a price belongs to a unit type, and every bed is bed. A yurt needs a bed in a single room to cost more than a bed in a six-bed room. The smallest change is to price a bed by its room's type, or by room capacity. That is an amendment to ADR 0038 and Seif decides it.

7. What to build, in order

Superseded as the working plan by yurt-first-plan.html, which orders the same items against the Backstage tasks. Kept here as what the audit recommended.

Ranked by what lets one real yurt stop using its old program. Every item goes through feature-design, and every answer here is proposed until Seif or Albaraa approves it explicitly.

Tier 0: before code (days, not weeks)

  1. Name a design-partner yurt. One operator whose contract, fee sheet and registration form we copy the shape of. Without one, Tier 1 item 1 is guesswork.
  2. Ask counsel two questions: the KBS or ministry reporting duty for private yurts, and which 2022 regulation clauses govern registration records, attendance and parent notification.
  3. A yurt demo Property on staging: blocks, gendered floors, rooms sold by bed, Residents on term contracts. It is cheap, it lets the dormitory journey be audited on hosted data at all, and it is the sales demo.
  4. Fix the false promise in the booking dialog (quick win): the Resident copy says "billed monthly" in all three languages, also in change-departure and close-day. Until billing exists it should say rent is not charged automatically. Not changed in this audit, because the honest wording depends on whether the desk is told to post rent by hand.

Tier 1: one yurt runs one month

  1. Term contract with an instalment schedule, posted by the worker on each due date, with an overdue state. This replaces RT-DEF-04 and is the yurt's income line.
  2. The student record: kimlik or passport number, nationality, gender, date of birth, university and year, veli name and phone, emergency contact (RG-DEF-07, CI-DEF-05). KVKK consent goes with it, because it is minors' and parents' data.
  3. Gender on a room or block, enforced when a bed is booked, ideally in the database, as a composite key or trigger in the house style.
  4. Bed board: a term-length calendar view (the window, not a new screen), beds-taken over beds-total on room cards, and an empty-beds count by block and gender.
  5. Portal activation: an invitation from the Stay sets user_id. Then show balance and next instalment (FO-DEF-11).
  6. The dormitory operating model from section 6: Today tiles, labels, booking defaults, term presets.

Tier 2: a yurt runs a whole term

  1. Messaging foundation, then SMS to the veli: overdue instalment, leave, an incident.
  2. Students raise requests and faults from the portal (MT-DEF-02); announcements with acknowledgement.
  3. Nightly attendance (yoklama) and leave requests (izin). Confirm with the design partner how they run them before designing.
  4. Stayover cleaning of occupied rooms (HK-DEF-01).
  5. Move-in week: bulk check-in, a move-in inspection, a deposit; move-out with damage charges and the deposit returned less deductions.
  6. e-Arşiv invoice per instalment, through Integrations.

Tier 3: next year

  1. Pre-registration and renewals for next term, a waiting list, and roommate placement.
  2. Visitor log, parcels, turnstile and card-access integration.
  3. Meal plans beyond "included".
  4. Summer short stays already work through the Guest flow. That mixed use is an edge over the yurt programs, so lead with it in sales.

8. Decisions for Seif or Albaraa

Each carries my recommendation and its reason. Reply "all", or the numbers to change.

  1. Is there a named yurt operator to design with? Recommend: yes, before Tier 1 starts. The fee and instalment shape varies between operators, and a guessed schedule is the most expensive thing to rework.
  2. Billing model: monthly rent, or a general instalment schedule? Recommend: the general schedule (N instalments, each with an amount and a due date). Monthly, quarterly and four senets are all one case of it. The Turkish yurt programs track senets; I have not checked how StarRez schedules its charges.
  3. How does the Property choose dormitory behaviour? Recommend: a dormitory operating model on the Property, set in Configuration, that only sets defaults and labels (section 6). ADR 0004 already rules out a fork.
  4. Turkish label for Resident in a dormitory? Recommend: "Öğrenci" in TR, "Student" in EN, "طالب" in AR, through a terminology mapping keyed by the operating model. Code and database keep Resident.
  5. Where does gender live? Recommend: on the room, with an optional default per building. Some private yurts are single-gender and some run separate blocks; a per-Property flag cannot express the second.
  6. Price of a bed? Recommend: by its room's capacity (single, double, triple...), as an amendment to ADR 0038. That is how yurt fee sheets are written.
  7. Are open-ended Resident stays allowed in a dormitory? Recommend: no; a term end date is required. Yurt contracts are fixed-term, and an open end has nothing to schedule instalments against. Keep open-ended for staff housing.
  8. Tier order against the 23 Oct plan, which put the hotel week first? Recommend: yurt Tier 1 first, keeping the items both share where they already overlap (identity capture, portal activation, the messaging foundation). Do only this if item 1 has a name, though. Otherwise do Tier 0 and the shared items and wait.
