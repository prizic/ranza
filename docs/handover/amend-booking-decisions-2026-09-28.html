Amending a booking and moving a Guest

The roadmap's next item, designed and not built. Fourteen decisions settle it; each has the answer I recommend, the alternative, and why. The full design is docs/features/amend-booking/ — three diagrams and fifty edge-case rows, every one proposed until you answer.

How to answer. "Approve all" makes every row in the table approved and lets building start. To change a decision, name its number and the answer you want; the rows it touches are listed under it.

It builds on rates (draft PR #86): what a changed booking costs is decisions 2–5.

  1. 01What does this feature cover?

    RecommendedThree slices, in order: change a booking that has not arrived (dates, Unit, or both); change an in-house Guest's planned departure (extend or shorten); move an in-house Guest to another Unit.
    AlternativeAlso drag-and-drop on the room calendar now.
    WhyThe commands and their refusals are the hard part; dragging only calls them. Blueprint 18.6 allows drag only when every rule permits the move, which is exactly what these commands decide.
    AB-S1-01, AB-S2-01, AB-S3-01, AB-DEF-01

  2. 02Does changing a booking's dates change its price?

    RecommendedNo. The price agreed with the Guest travels with the booking.
    AlternativeRe-quote at today's price list on every change.
    WhyA Guest who moves their stay by a day expects the price they were given; re-pricing would punish a date change with a surprise. It also matches ADR 0038: the price is fixed when booked.
    AB-S1-04

  3. 03Moving a booking to a different kind of Unit (room → suite)?

    RecommendedIt takes today's price for the new kind, shown old → new before saving, and the price guard refuses the save if the price moved while the dialog was open.
    AlternativeKeep the booked price.
    WhyA different kind of Unit is a different product; keeping a room price for a suite would be an unplanned upgrade on every such change. Showing it before saving keeps the desk honest with the Guest.
    AB-S1-03

  4. 04Moving an in-house Guest to a different kind of Unit?

    RecommendedKeep the booked price. An upgrade the Guest pays for is posted as a Folio charge, which already works.
    AlternativeRe-price the remaining nights like a booking change.
    WhyA mid-stay move is usually the hotel's decision — a fault, an upgrade offered — and silently raising the Guest's price would be wrong far more often than right.
    AB-S3-05, AB-DEF-05

  5. 05Extra nights when a stay is extended?

    RecommendedAt the booking's own price, charged as each night closes.
    AlternativeAt today's price for the kind.
    WhySame reasoning as (2): the Guest agreed a nightly price; an extension is more of the same stay.
    AB-S2-01

  6. 06How is history kept?

    RecommendedEvery change appends a revision row (reservation_changes): what the dates, Unit and price were and became, the business date, the reason, and who. Never updated or deleted, shown on the booking's history and the audit log.
    AlternativeThe audit log alone.
    WhyBlueprint 7.4 asks for revision records, and a booking's own history (Guest 360, the timeline) should be readable without the audit screen's permission.
    AB-S1-24

  7. 07Who may change bookings and stays?

    RecommendedOne new permission, front_desk.amend, on Owner, Manager and Front Desk.
    AlternativeSeparate permissions for amending and for room moves.
    WhyThe same people do all three at a desk. Splitting is cheap later if an Organization asks; one permission keeps the People screen simple now.
    AB-S1-21

  8. 08Does a room move need a reason?

    RecommendedYes: the room has a fault, an upgrade, the Guest asked, or other with a note. A booking change and an extension take an optional note.
    AlternativeNever ask.
    WhyMoves are where disputes and maintenance patterns show up; a reason costs a click and makes the history useful.
    AB-S3-02

  9. 09What happens to rooms and the Folio on a move?

    RecommendedOne Stay, one Folio, the same price; the Unit changes from now. The new room must be in service and ready (as a check-in); the room left becomes dirty through the worker (as a check-out).
    AlternativeEnd the Stay and open a new one in the new room.
    WhyA new Stay would split the bill into two Folios and break the room-night history for one visit. Changing the Unit on the same Stay keeps the Guest's visit whole.
    AB-S3-01, AB-S3-03, AB-S3-08

  10. 10Can a readiness check be overridden for an urgent move?

    RecommendedNot in this slice: a move waits for a ready room, as a check-in does.
    AlternativeA manager override with a reason.
    WhyKeeps one rule for 'is this room ready for somebody'. An override is easy to add once there is a manager-review flow.
    AB-S3-03, AB-DEF-04

  11. 11Shortening a stay to today?

    RecommendedNot an amendment — leaving today is a check-out, which already asks about early departure and reviews the bill. A shortened departure is tomorrow or later.
    AlternativeAllow it and leave the check-out for later.
    WhyOne path for leaving; no stay that ends today without being checked out.
    AB-S2-03, AB-S2-04

  12. 12What about bookings whose arrival has passed, and early arrivals?

    RecommendedA passed arrival can move to today or later only; an early arrival moves to today and is then checked in normally.
    AlternativeAllow any dates.
    WhyKeeps closed days closed (RZ001) and makes 'the Guest is here a day early' a two-click flow.
    AB-S1-09, AB-S1-11

  13. 13Where do the actions appear?

    RecommendedReservations: Change booking. Arrivals: Change for early or late arrivals. Departures and the room calendar's drawer: Extend, Shorten, Move. Close the day: Extend beside a Guest past departure. A maintenance warning links each affected booking to Change.
    AlternativeOne central 'edit booking' page only.
    WhyThe desk changes things from where the problem shows up; the same dialog opens everywhere.
    AB-S1-27, AB-S2-05, AB-S1-17

  14. 14Not now?

    RecommendedDragging on the calendar, split stays and group bookings, moving to another Property, changing who the Guest is, per-Guest price overrides.
    Alternative—
    WhyEach is its own decision; none is needed to amend, extend or move.
