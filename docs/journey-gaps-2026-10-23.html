What a hotel and a dormitory do every day, and what Ranza cannot do yet

Verdict. The hotel core is deep and correct: a booking becomes a Stay, nights are charged, payments are recorded, check-out dirties the room and housekeeping turns it. What stops a real hotel running one week on Ranza is the work around that core: the Guest as a person the desk can manage, charges the desk posts itself, legal registration at check-in, a receipt for the Guest, cleaning of occupied rooms, and any message leaving the system. The dormitory product barely exists: a Resident can be booked open-ended, but nothing bills them, nothing lets them into the Portal, and none of the long-stay workflows exist.

Decide this first. The blueprint serves hotels and dormitories equally. The code is hotel-shaped: roughly four fifths of the hotel journey is walkable and about a sixth of the dormitory one. This repository began as a dormitory pilot, so if that customer is still the target, the dormitory gaps in section 4 are the blockers, not the hotel ones. My recommendation: finish the hotel week (Tier 1) first, because it is the shorter road to a property running live. Pull Resident Portal activation (a small change) into it anyway, because every Portal feature already built or planned is unreachable without it. If Seif has a dormitory customer waiting, swap Tier 1 and Tier 3.

4. The dormitory journey, stage by stage

A dormitory's unit of work is a Resident on a contract for a term, billed monthly, who lives there for months and interacts mostly through the Portal. Ranza models the Resident as a Stay type, and beds as Units under a room (ADR 0025). That part is right. Almost nothing past it exists.

Stage What a dormitory does Ranza today
Application and allocation Take applications for a term, keep a waiting list, apply rules (gender per floor, year, room type), place roommates, renew last year's Residents. Missing entirely. A Property has no type and no term or academic year exists VERIFIED. Booking a bed open-ended is the only path.
Contract Term dates, monthly rent, security deposit, house rules acknowledged, a guardian for a minor, an emergency contact. Missing. No contract, no deposit FO-DEF-03, no guardian or emergency contact RG-DEF-07.
Move-in Room inspection and inventory signed, keys handed over, registration with the authorities. Built: check-in works for a Resident. Missing: inspection and inventory, identity capture. Whether private dormitories in Türkiye have the same reporting duty as hotels UNKNOWN; ask Seif or counsel.
Monthly life Bill rent each month, chase late payers, take requests, post announcements, record nights out and visitors, accept parcels, serve meals. Rent is never billed. Residents are excluded from room nights and monthly billing is deferred RT-DEF-04, CD-S3-01. A dormitory on Ranza has no income line.

5. Cross-cutting gaps that hurt every journey

  • No messages leave the system. No email, no notification table, nothing consumes the outbox for people VERIFIED. Booking confirmations, a Resident's invitation and a maintenance alert all need the same minimal transactional email first.
  • No global search or command palette, which blueprint 4.6 puts in the shell VERIFIED. Each list searches itself well OBSERVED 22 OCT, but a desk answering a phone call wants one box.
  • Going live needs an import. A hotel switching systems carries months of future bookings. Rooms can be added in bulk, but there is no import for Guests or future Reservations, and past Stays are refused by design CD-DEF-08. Blueprint 19.1 makes import a product workflow.
  • A second customer needs a Control Plane. Organizations and Entitlements are written by SQL today OA-DEF-04.
  • Channel manager. Phase 5, and correctly so for a pilot. Any hotel taking most of its bookings from OTAs will ask for it on the first sales call, though INDUSTRY. It is not a dormitory concern.

6. Existing flows that need improving

From the 22 Oct test, ranked by how much they cost the desk's trust OBSERVED 22 OCT. Some may have moved since: d86b3ad and 172ef41 touched arrivals, departures and the backlog. Re-check before ticketing.

  1. "What is free tonight" has four answers (Today, Rooms, calendar, Housekeeping), and the unit picker offered a room still occupied until checkout. Define each count once, label it, and show readiness in the picker.
  2. The duplicate-email match is silent. Ask "Existing Guest X matched by email: update the phone?" before saving.
  3. Today does not drive the shift. Arrivals belong in the ordered list with a Check in button, and finished rows should grey out. PR #92 (four layouts) is open and may change this.
  4. Change booking hides the money. Show the old total, the new total and the difference before saving.
  5. Cancel and no-show are one-way and free. Add a reinstate with a reason. Add a no-show fee setting, or state plainly that none exists.
  6. Money dialogs fail silently on 0 and negative amounts, and the payment dialog prefilled the previous amount, not the balance.
  7. Tablet width hides balance and status behind a sticky action column, exactly where Check out is pressed.

7. What to build, in order

Ranked by what removes the most parallel notebooks per unit of work. Each item still goes through feature-design and Seif's approval, and every answer here is proposed.

Tier 1: a hotel can run one real week

  1. The Guest the desk can manage. A Guests page with search, edit and history, a confirmed duplicate match, and party size (adults and children) plus a note on the booking. This closes RG-DEF-06 and the first step of RG-DEF-09.
  2. The front desk posts charges. Grant finance.post_charge to the shipped front desk role, and add a small charge catalogue per Property (item, price, category). The catalogue also unblocks AN-DEF-01. Cheap.
  3. Registration at check-in. Identity document, nationality and date of birth, recorded at check-in or flagged "ID missing", plus a KBS export or integration. This is legal: start the counsel question now, because it is the slowest item.
  4. Something to hand the Guest. A printable or PDF folio and receipt now. e-Arşiv later, through Integrations.
  5. Stayover cleaning and assignment. A daily status for occupied rooms, and rooms assigned to named housekeepers on a phone-first queue. This closes HK-DEF-01 and HK-DEF-04, and blueprint stories 7 and 8.
  6. Check-in and check-out times on the Property. These unlock early check-in, late check-out, and an "overdue" that means today rather than yesterday.
  7. One transactional email: a booking confirmation. It lays the delivery foundation the Resident invitation reuses.
  8. Taxes on room nights, meaning VAT and the accommodation tax. Without them every figure in the books is wrong. Needs an accountant INDUSTRY.
  9. Resident Portal activation, pulled forward from Tier 3. An invitation from the Stay sets user_id. It is a small change, and without it the Portal has no users.

Tier 2: the desk stops keeping a parallel notebook

  1. Deposits and guarantees, refunds, split folios and company billing (FO-DEF-03 and FO-DEF-05).
  2. Shift handover notes and a cashier shift close with a cash count.
  3. Reopen a closed day with a reason (CD-DEF-02 and CD-DEF-03).
  4. Reinstate a cancelled booking; a no-show fee setting.
  5. Booking source on every Reservation, and group bookings covering several rooms.
  6. A global search box and a Guest 360 drawer.
  7. Import of Guests and future Reservations, with preview and a row-level error report.
  8. A Control Plane, at minimum to create an Organization and its Entitlements without SQL.

Tier 3: the dormitory product (Tier 1 if a dormitory customer comes first)

  1. Contract and term: dates, monthly rent and deposit, attached to the Resident's booking.
  2. Monthly billing (RT-DEF-04): the worker posts rent at each period start. Pro-rata for a part month. Late-payment state.
  3. Portal: balance (FO-DEF-11), requests and maintenance reports (MT-DEF-02), announcements with acknowledgement.
  4. Move-in and move-out inspection with damage charges and deposit return.
  5. Guardian and emergency contact (RG-DEF-07).
  6. Allocation: applications, waiting list, placement rules, renewal for next term.
  7. Night-out permission, visitors and parcels. These are common in Turkish dormitories INDUSTRY; confirm with Seif which ones the target customer needs before designing any.

Tier 4: later, as the roadmap already says

Channel manager, payment gateway, door locks, F&B and meal plans, inventory and procurement, revenue management. No change to the roadmap's order is proposed here.
