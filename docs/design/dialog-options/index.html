Four designs for the Ranza dialogs

Each is shown on the two dialogs that stress it most: check-out, which has a bill and a decision, and move guest, which is a plain form. Green and white only. Names and figures are sample data. Nothing here is built yet; this is a choice.

My pick: A, Ledger.It is the sleekest, it is the only one that works unchanged for every dialog from a two-field cancel to the new-booking form, and the amount reading as a large light numeral is what the reference designs do. Second is B for the big dialogs. I would not choose D, and C only for check-out and change departure.

Option

A · Ledger

The amount is the headline.

One narrow column. Who it is, then the one figure that matters in large light numerals, then what is asked of the desk. The same skeleton fits every dialog: subject, one fact or figure, fields, footer.

Trade-off. Fastest to read and the easiest to roll out to all 36 dialogs. Gives up the big illustration (kept small, at the end of the subject line).

Option

B · Editorial split

Type on deep green, work on white.

A near-black emerald panel carries the subject in large type and the facts in a short list; the work sits on white beside it. No drawings: the typography is the design.

Trade-off. The most striking and the most premium. Needs width, so it is for the larger dialogs only; small confirms would keep a single column.

Option

C · Timeline sheet

A side sheet that shows where they are in the stay.

A sheet from the edge instead of a centred modal. The stay is drawn as a track (arrived, today, planned departure), so leaving early is seen, not read. Avatar initials identify the Guest.

Trade-off. The most useful for check-out and change departure. Wrong for tiny confirms (cancel, undo), and it changes how dialogs feel across the app.

Option

D · Statement

The bill as a statement, with dotted leaders.

A pale panel holds the figures as a printed statement: label, dotted leader, amount, a total under a double rule. The form sits beneath, on white.

Trade-off. Distinctive and fitting for money. It is check-out specific: the move dialog has little to put on a statement, which the second mock shows.
