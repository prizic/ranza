Front desk · Today · design directions

Four ways to answer "what do I do next?"

The current Today page is a grid of equal tiles. A desk agent does not think in tiles. They think in time (who is coming, who is late), in rooms (what can I give this guest), and in decisions (what is blocking me). Each direction below leads with one of those.

What is wrong with the current page, from the 2026-10-11 audit

  • Every tile has the same weight, so the one overdue guest looks like the one quiet card (TODAY-08).
  • Two tiles share a title and a number and read as a duplicate (TODAY-11).
  • "Ready rooms" can count a room with a guest in it (TODAY-02).
  • Nothing is organised around rooms, which is how a desk decides where to put someone.
  • The 8 unclosed days sit in the same visual register as a routine notice.
1

Shift timeline

"Who is coming, who is leaving, and are the rooms ready in time?"

The day as a horizontal line with a NOW marker. Departures on one lane, arrivals on the other, each guest placed at the time on their booking. Late and overdue stretch back to where they should have been. Every arrival shows whether its room is ready, so risk is visible two hours early. Below it, a ranked "Do these in order" list.

Strongest at
Shift planning, spotting trouble before it arrives
Weakest at
Arrivals with no expected time (they fall off the line)

House map

"Which room can I give this guest right now?"

Every room as a tile, grouped by floor, with its state shown by colour, icon, text and hatch pattern. Select an arrival and the map answers: the assigned room is ringed, other ready rooms of the same type say "Can take", the rest fade. Select a room for its actions. The filter chips are the counts.

Strongest at
Walk-ins, room moves, the "room not ready" problem
Weakest at
Time. It does not show who is late, only which rooms
Open → 3

Next up

"Just tell me the one thing to do now."

One large card for the most urgent item, with a plain checklist of what is blocking it and the button that fixes it. A ranked queue beside it; the arrows step through. Arriving, In house and Leaving sit underneath as three short lanes.

Strongest at
A quiet, junior or solo desk. Fewest decisions
Weakest at
A rush. One card at a time hides the other fifteen
Open → 4

Night desk

"What happened today, and what do I hand over?"

A dark evening layout at 22:47. How the house moved today as a waterfall (12 at the start, 6 left, 6 arrived, 1 still expected, 13 tonight). The unclosed days with the oldest first, the next seven nights, tomorrow's room readiness and a four-item handover list.

Strongest at
Closing the day, handover, the night auditor's job
Weakest at
Daytime. It is retrospective, not operational

My pick: build 1, borrow from the rest

Build the shift timeline as Today for Front Desk. Its question, "what is about to go wrong?", is the one the current page cannot answer, and it needs no new data: expectedArrivalTime and the planned departure are already on the booking. Take the ranked list from 3 (already inside 1) and the room-ready check on each arrival from 2.

Do not build 3 as a separate page. A single hero card is the right idea for a quiet desk and the wrong one on a busy afternoon, when the desk needs the whole day in view.

Two things worth deciding

  • The map (2) belongs on its own route, probably as the default view of Front Office, not on Today. It is the best room-first view and it would make Today too heavy.
  • The night desk (4) is a mode, not a role. The same Today page switches to it after a set hour, since the 8 unclosed days and the handover list matter most then. That needs one product decision: which hour, and per Property.
  • Arrivals with no expected time have nowhere to sit on a timeline. Today they get a line under it; if that is common in practice, it needs a tray of its own.

What these previews are not · Demo data: guest names, balances, tomorrow's arrivals, guest requests and the seven-night outlook are invented for the preview, the figures are frozen at 14:32 (22:47 for the night desk), and the sidebar is left out. Geist is not bundled, so a system font stands in. Checked at 1480 px only; narrow widths and Arabic RTL are not designed yet (the timeline places guests by absolute position and would need a mirrored axis).
