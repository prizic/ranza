Yurt first: one interface, a dormitory mode, and the order to build it in

The plan. Ranza serves Turkish student dormitories (yurt) first, through the same product a hotel uses. A Property gets an operating model, hotel or dormitory, which changes what the screens default to, show first and call things, and never the rules underneath (ADR 0057). The first milestone is one yurt runs one month on Ranza: students registered, beds placed by gender, instalments charged and chased, students in the portal. The second is a whole term. Everything below is tracked in Backstage as RANZ-103..113, alongside the dormitory tasks already filed as RANZ-78..101.

What is agreed and what is not. On 31 Oct Albaraa agreed the direction: yurt first, one interface with a dormitory mode, no separate product. That agreement is recorded here; it is not yet an approval of the ADR or of the decisions in section 6, which stay proposed until Seif or Albaraa approves them by number, on the pull request or on RANZ-103 (CLAUDE.local.md, "Who approves"). Every feature still goes through feature-design before code.

1. Why this, in three lines

The 31 Oct audit found the screens well built and hotel-shaped, and three blockers that are not visual: nothing bills a Resident (RT-DEF-04), the student record is a name, an email and a phone, and no screen thinks in beds over a term (calendar capped at 30 days, one name per room card, one price for every bed). A Resident also cannot sign in to the portal, because nothing writes stays.user_id (GP-DEF-02). All of it was re-checked on origin/main 897d7fd0 for this plan.

2. The shape: one interface, two operating models

The same for every Property

  • Check-in, check-out, moves, extensions
  • Folios, payments, reversals, the ledger
  • Housekeeping status, maintenance, requests
  • Roles, permissions, row-level security, audit, export
  • Every database rule and every test of one

What a dormitory Property changes

  • Words: Öğrenci, Kayıt, Boş yatak, Öğrenci talepleri (TR/EN/AR)
  • Today: empty beds, overdue instalments, leave, faults
  • Booking: starts as a student, term dates, student and veli fields
  • Calendar: opens on the term; room cards show beds 3/4
  • Rail: dormitory work first; Close the day further down

The guard rail: no SQL policy, trigger or function may read the operating model, and a pgTAP assertion says so. A rule a yurt needs (gender on a room, instalments, a required term end, attendance) is built as a setting or capability any Property can turn on; the model only chooses its default. This keeps one product, and a hostel or staff housing later gets the same rules without a third model.

3. Done means

Milestone A: one yurt runs one month

On the yurt demo Property, on hosted staging, in Turkish, English and Arabic:

  1. A manager switches the Property to dormitory and every screen reads the dormitory words; switching back changes no record.
  2. The desk registers a student with identity document, gender, date of birth, university, veli and emergency contact, for a term, into a bed in a room that admits them; the database refuses one that does not.
  3. Each student's instalments are scheduled when they are registered and posted to their Folio on each due date by the worker, without anyone touching it.
  4. Today shows empty beds by block and gender and the instalments overdue, and each figure opens its rows.
  5. The room calendar shows a ten-month booking whole, and room cards show beds taken over beds total.
  6. A student signs in to the portal from an invitation and sees their bed, term, balance and next instalment.
  7. pnpm check, pnpm db:test (local and hosted) and pnpm test:browser pass, and every boundary claim has been sabotaged once and gone red.

Milestone B: a yurt runs a whole term

Milestone A, plus: messages to the veli, students raising requests and faults from the portal, announcements, nightly attendance and leave, weekly cleaning of occupied rooms, move-in week and move-out with deposit, and an invoice per instalment.

5. How each task is run

The house process, unchanged: feature-design grill with a recommendation on every question, then diagrams and docs/features/<feature>/edge-cases.csv rows written as proposed, then approval by number, then the build in its own worktree and database, then evidence (pgTAP with a sabotage run for every boundary, a browser spec for every screen, hosted db:test after deploy). Every screen ships in Turkish, English and Arabic with logical directions, and its handoff ends with the UX tweaks made and UX proposals lines.

6. Decisions open on RANZ-103

# Question Recommendation and why Status
1 Design-partner yurt first? Yes. Fee shapes differ between operators, and billing is the costliest thing to rework. proposed
2 Billing model A general instalment schedule (N payments, amount and due date each). Monthly and four senets are both cases of it; the Turkish yurt programs track senets. proposed
3 How a Property becomes a dormitory An operating model on the Property that sets defaults and words only (ADR 0057). ADR 0004 already rules out a fork. proposed
4 Resident's label in a dormitory Öğrenci / Student / طالب through the typed catalogue (ICU select); code and database keep Resident. proposed
5 Where gender lives On the room, with an optional default per building: some yurts are single-gender, some run separate blocks. proposed
6 Price of a bed By its room's capacity, as an amendment to ADR 0038. That is how yurt fee sheets are written. proposed
7 Open-ended Resident stays in a dormitory Not allowed; a term end is required, because instalments need an end to schedule against. Staff housing keeps open-ended. proposed
8 Order against the 23 Oct hotel-first plan Yurt Stage 1 first, keeping what both share (identity, portal access, messaging). If decision 1 has no name within two weeks, fall back to the shared items only. proposed
