Front Office: lived-experience UX audit

Thursday 22 October 2026, about 14:00 Istanbul. Hosted staging (ranza.46-225-168-54.nip.io, build abc47da, the build that carries the 2026-10-11 audit fixes). Persona: a Front Desk agent at Demo Otel İstanbul (42 rooms), signed in as the Front Desk test account. Mode: audit only, nothing was persisted.

How far this audit goes. Every read, search, filter, dialog and form was exercised on the real app. Every valid submit (check in, check out, payment, booking, close a day) was stopped at the confirm step, because a check-in or a closed day cannot be undone on shared staging. What happens after those buttons is judged from the code and the feature docs and marked inferred. Findings that depend on the demo data (13 overdue stays, 11 unclosed days, every booking "No price") are marked data-dependent. Items I saw once are marked "seen once"; the rest were seen on two or more loads. I could not check the Today layouts other than Timeline, because choosing one saves a preference.

1. Verdict

  1. A trained desk agent can run an ordinary shift unaided: find a guest, check in, extend, move, check out, see the room board. Everything loads in about a second, search forgives typos and phone formats, and Arabic mirrors correctly. Nothing found blocks work outright.
  2. The biggest risk is money at check-out. The dialog says the guest owes TRY 5,470, but taking payment means opening the Folio in another tab. The cursor starts in the "why the balance stays open" box, so the easiest path is to check out unpaid with a typed reason. After paying in the other tab, the open dialog probably refuses the confirm because the bill changed (inferred, CO-S1-12).
  3. The second risk is the answer to "what can I sell right now?" There are five different figures across Today, Rooms, the calendar and Housekeeping, and none of the room pickers shows which free rooms are actually clean.
  4. The third is bookings that never arrived. They sit on no work list until Close the day raises them days later, one day at a time.
  5. For daily friction: the guest row on Arrivals and Departures cannot be opened, the Front Office rail group starts collapsed, there is no search outside Reservations, and on a tablet or phone the room and readiness columns scroll out of sight before the agent presses Check in.

4. Top 10 changes, ranked by time saved × how often

  1. I can take the money and check the guest out in one dialog. (FO-01) Every departure, 20+ a day.
  2. One number tells me what I can sell now, and the room lists show which free rooms are clean. (FO-03, FO-04, FO-05) Every walk-in and every phone enquiry.
  3. Guests who never arrived show up on Arrivals with check in, no-show and cancel next to them. (FO-02) Stops inventory and revenue drifting for days.
  4. I can open any guest from Arrivals or Departures and see their balance and open requests. (FO-07, FO-08) Every check-in and check-out.
  5. Today and Arrivals agree on who is late, and an arrival blocked by a dirty room outranks old overdue departures. (FO-06)
  6. On a tablet or phone I still see room, readiness and lateness before I press Check in. (FO-15)
  7. I can close a backlog of days once, with one reason, and see what holds each day. (FO-09)
  8. Arrivals, Departures and Reservations are one click away, and I can search for a guest from anywhere. (FO-14)
  9. I can start a booking by clicking a free room on the calendar. (FO-13)
  10. The same word means the same thing everywhere (booking/reservation, room/unit, sentence case). (FO-10, FO-11)

5. Inconsistencies with sibling screens

  • Lateness: Today says "in 2h 31m", Arrivals says "3 days late" for the same booking (FO-06).
  • Availability figures: Today, Rooms, calendar and Housekeeping disagree (FO-03). Housekeeping's "Dirty 14" counts occupied rooms; Today's "4 dirty" counts vacant ones. The scopes are not labelled.
  • Overdue treatment: the calendar marks overdue guests amber; the Rooms board does not (FO-05).
  • Opening a guest: Reservations and the calendar open a detail view; Arrivals and Departures do nothing on click (FO-07).
  • Readiness: Arrivals and Today show it per arrival; the Rooms board and the Unit picker do not (FO-04, FO-05).
  • Action wording: "Check out" (rows) vs "Confirm check-out" (dialog) vs "Keep in house" vs "Close"; Guest Experience uses "Start Working / Resolve / Cancel request" in Title Case. "Cancel request" sits at the same weight as "Resolve" in the row, one click from a destructive action.
  • Back button: present on all front-office pages as ADR 0035 requires. The Folio opened from the check-out dialog has a back arrow but no route back to the check-out it came from.

6. Not built or needs backend work (not UX defects)

  • Identity capture and the police arrival and departure notification (KBS): deferred (CI-DEF-05, CO-S1-33, "required before launch"). Check-in today is one button with no ID, signature, deposit or key step. This is the largest functional gap for a Turkish front desk.
  • Deposits, taxes, discounts, credits, refunds, split folios and company billing: deferred (FO-DEF-03, FO-DEF-05, FP-DEF-02). A payment is a record only: "no card is charged".
  • Adding a charge as Front Desk: the Add charge form exists but is hidden for this role with no explanation on the page. That follows the role rules, but a guest disputing a minibar line cannot be helped at the desk. Pending the owner decision RANZ-56.
  • Guest requests on Today and Departures: proposed, not built (TD-S6-01). Request #3 "Late check-out at 14:00" is invisible where it matters.
  • Check in from the calendar drawer: RC-DEF-04. Saved views: RC-DEF-02. Reopening a closed day: the dialog says it is not available yet.
  • Booking from a free calendar cell (FO-13) and a one-step "walk-in and check in": in neither the built nor the deferred rows; they need a design row first.
  • Rates on staging: every booking says "No price" because no rate is set for the unit types. This is configuration, but it makes the money screens look broken to anyone auditing with this data.

7. What works well and must not regress

  • Speed: Today, Arrivals, Departures and Reservations all had content in about 0.8–1.5 s. The browser console was clean on the one screen I checked (Departures); I did not check the others.
  • Search forgives diacritics, phone formats, case and multi-word queries, across guest, room, reference, phone and date.
  • The check-in guard asks before putting a guest into a dirty room ("Check in anyway", by the code) and re-reads readiness on the server.
  • The check-out dialog names its consequence before the click, including that the room is marked dirty, that the folio stays open with the amount, and that the reason is recorded. Close the day says that reopening is not available yet.
  • The calendar's accessibility is unusually good: every bar is a button with a full spoken label (state, dates, "still in the room tonight") and a stated alternative to dragging ("use Move… in its details").
  • Unit picker greys taken rooms and says why ("Someone is staying then", "Booked for these nights"); the stay-date picker disables past dates.
  • Row-level actions on Departures cover the real jobs (Open folio, Show on the room map, Change departure, Move Guest).
  • Arabic is fully translated and mirrors correctly, including the rail and table order. Skip-to-content link exists; tab stops to the first Check in are 28 in the whole page, 9 after the skip link.
  • Housekeeping orders rooms by what the desk needs first (arrival today, leaving today) and carries "Report a problem" on every row.

8. What I did not test

  • Any submit that persists (see the note at the top). The after-submit behaviour of check-in, check-out, payment, new booking, change booking and close day is read from code, not observed.
  • Empty and very large data: the property has 42 rooms and 40 reservations, so "empty" and "thousands" are inferred from the empty-state components and the page size of 10 and 50, not observed.
  • Turkish locale, dark mode, other roles (Manager, Finance, Housekeeping), and the four other Today layouts (House map, Next up, Night desk, Overview).
  • Keyboard: I counted tab stops and read the focus order; I did not drive a full check-in by keyboard.
